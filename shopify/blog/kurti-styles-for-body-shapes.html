The right kurti is not about hiding anything. It is about choosing a cut that feels comfortable and makes you feel like yourself. Different silhouettes fall differently on different bodies, so knowing a few simple guidelines makes online shopping much easier. Here is a friendly guide to kurti styles for common Indian body shapes, plus tips on necklines, sleeves and length.

These are suggestions, not rules. If you love a style, wear it.

First, know the main kurti cuts

  • Straight cut: falls straight from the shoulder to the hem. Clean and easy, with side slits.
  • A-line: fitted at the top and gently wider towards the hem, like the letter A.
  • Anarkali: fitted to just under the bust, then opens into a full, gathered flare.
  • Kali or flared: made of several panels (kalis) that add a graceful swing from the waist down.
  • Shirt-style or collared: a collar and button placket for a sharper, more formal look.
  • Short kurti and tunic: hip-length or a little longer, easy to pair with pants and jeans.

Pear shape (hips wider than bust)

Your goal: balance the top and bottom.

  • Best cuts: A-line, Anarkali and kali styles that skim over the hips without clinging.
  • Draw the eye up: choose yoke embroidery, mirror work or detailed necklines like V-necks and boat necks.
  • Length: knee-length or longer kurtis flow over the hips beautifully.
  • Bottoms: straight or relaxed pants in a darker or plain colour.

Apple shape (fuller around the middle)

Your goal: a smooth line through the middle and a lengthening effect.

  • Best cuts: straight-cut and gently A-line kurtis in soft, flowing fabrics like mul cotton, which fall without clinging.
  • Necklines: V-necks and deep round necks lengthen the upper body.
  • Details: vertical lines, button plackets and long embroidered panels draw the eye up and down.
  • Avoid: tight waist belts and very stiff fabrics around the middle.

Hourglass (bust and hips balanced, defined waist)

Your goal: show off your waist without losing comfort.

  • Best cuts: Anarkali and kali styles that are fitted at the top, and kurtis with a tie-up or gathered waist.
  • Straight kurtis work too: choose ones with side slits and soft fabric so they follow your shape.
  • Avoid: very boxy, oversized cuts that hide your waist, unless that is the relaxed look you want.

Rectangle (bust, waist and hips similar)

Your goal: add shape and movement.

  • Best cuts: Anarkalis, kali and flared kurtis, and angrakha-style wrap fronts that create curves.
  • Details: gathers, pintucks, tiers and layered dupattas add volume in the right places.
  • Prints: bold hand-block prints and Leheriya stripes add energy to simple cuts.

Try: the Olive Green Jaipuri Leheriya 3-Piece Suit Set or the Rich Brown Bandhani Anarkali Set.

Inverted triangle (broad shoulders, narrower hips)

Your goal: soften the shoulders and add volume below.

  • Best cuts: A-line, Anarkali and flared kurtis that widen towards the hem.
  • Necklines: V-necks and deep U-necks break up the shoulder line. Avoid very wide boat necks and heavy shoulder embroidery.
  • Sleeves: simple straight ¾ sleeves rather than puffed or balloon sleeves.
  • Bottoms: palazzos, flared or relaxed pants add balance.

Petite (around 5'2" / 157 cm or shorter)

  • Choose kurtis that end at or just above the knee rather than at the ankle.
  • Matching kurta and pant colours, like co-ord sets, create one long line that adds height.
  • Small and medium prints suit a petite frame better than very large motifs.
  • Short kurtis and tunics with straight pants look neat and modern.

Tall (around 5'7" / 170 cm or taller)

  • Long kurtis (around 46 in / 117 cm) and floor-length Anarkalis look graceful on you.
  • Contrast colours between kurta and pants break up the length nicely.
  • Layering works well: try a long shrug over a kurta or co-ord, like the Kutchi Patchwork Long Shrug.
  • Check sleeve length on the product page, since ¾ sleeves may sit higher on longer arms.

Plus size

  • Choose soft, breathable fabrics like mul cotton and cotton that drape rather than cling.
  • Straight, A-line and Anarkali cuts with structure at the shoulders all look lovely. Make sure the kurti fits well across the bust and shoulders.
  • V-necks and button plackets add length to the upper body.
  • Don't size up too much: an oversized kurti can look bulkier than one that fits. Our charts go up to 3XL (44 in bust), and if you are between sizes, choose the larger one.
