Hand-block printed cotton is one of the most comfortable things you can wear in India's weather: soft, breathable and full of colour. Because the colour is pressed into the fabric by hand, often with natural or traditional dyes, it needs a little more care than a factory-printed T-shirt, especially in the first few washes. Follow these simple steps and your Jaipuri, Ajrakh, Dabu or Bagru prints will stay beautiful for years.

Why hand-block prints need gentle care

  • Extra dye can bleed at first. Hand printing leaves some colour sitting on the surface of the fibres. It washes away in the first few washes, which is normal and not a defect.
  • Cotton and mul are fine fibres. Mul (mulmul) is woven from very fine cotton yarn, which makes it soft and airy but also more delicate.
  • Hot water and strong detergents fade colour. Heat, bleach and harsh chemicals break down dyes faster and can cause cotton to shrink.

The first wash

  1. Wash it alone. Keep a new block-printed piece away from other clothes, especially whites and light colours.
  2. Use cold water. Room-temperature or cold water is gentlest on both the dye and the fabric.
  3. Do a quick rinse first. Dip the garment in plain cold water for a few minutes. If the water turns coloured, rinse once or twice more until it runs mostly clear before washing with detergent.
  4. Don't soak it for hours. Long soaks, especially overnight, can let loose dye settle back into the lighter parts of the print.

Many families add a spoon of salt or a little white vinegar to the first rinse. It may help a little with some dyes, but it does not permanently "fix" colour, so cold water and washing separately are still the most important steps.

Everyday washing

  • Choose a mild detergent. A gentle liquid detergent works best. Avoid bleach, strong stain removers and detergents with "whitening" agents, which fade prints.
  • Hand wash gently. Swirl and squeeze the fabric softly. Don't scrub the print with a brush or rub it hard against itself.
  • Machine wash only if you must. After the first few washes, you can use the gentle or delicate cycle with cold water. Turn the garment inside out and put it in a laundry bag.
  • Wash dark and light prints separately for the first several washes, and wash matching pieces of a set together so their colour fades evenly.
  • Never wring. Twisting stretches the fibres. Press the water out gently, or roll the garment in a dry towel.

Drying

  • Dry in the shade. Strong Indian sun fades block-printed colours quickly. Dry indoors, on a shaded balcony or in the evening.
  • Turn it inside out so that any light that does reach it falls on the reverse.
  • Hang it straight. Shake it out and hang it on a hanger or flat so it dries without deep creases.
  • Avoid the tumble dryer. Heat can shrink cotton and dull the colours.

Ironing

  • Iron on the reverse side on a medium (cotton) setting.
  • It is easiest while the fabric is still slightly damp. For mul, a lower setting or a thin cloth on top works well.
  • Keep the iron away from mirror work, tassels, pearls and lace. Iron around them, or place a cloth over them.

Stains

Treat a stain as soon as you can. Rinse the spot from the back with cold water and dab it with a little mild detergent. Avoid bleach and strong stain removers, which can take the print off along with the stain. For oily or stubborn stains on a favourite piece, a good dry cleaner is safer than scrubbing.

Storage

  • Store clean, completely dry garments folded in a cotton or muslin bag rather than plastic, so the fabric can breathe.
  • Keep them away from direct sunlight in the wardrobe.
  • Refold pieces you don't wear often every few months, so the fold lines don't fade or weaken.
  • Neem leaves or a cedar block keep insects away better than mothballs, which leave a strong smell.

Notes for specific prints

  • Indigo and Dabu: deep indigo can rub off a little at first, so wash separately for longer and avoid wearing a brand-new indigo piece with light-coloured bags or dupattas on its first outing.
  • Ajrakh: traditionally dyed Ajrakh can have a faint earthy smell when new. It fades after a few washes. Read more in What is Ajrakh Printing?
  • Leheriya and Bandhani: treat these tie-dye pieces the same way. See Leheriya and Bandhani Explained.
  • Prints with embroidery: follow the gentler of the two care routines. Our Chikankari care guide covers hand embroidery.
  • Silk prints: dry clean only.

The beauty of handmade

Hand-block prints soften and mellow beautifully over time. Small variations in colour and print are part of the handmade process, not flaws. With gentle care, your piece will only become more comfortable with every wear.
